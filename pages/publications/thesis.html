Research Overview

Understanding Battery Heat Generation Under Realistic EV Operation

Accurate estimation of heat generation is essential for the design of efficient Battery Thermal Management Systems, battery safety and reliable electric vehicle operation.

Lithium-ion batteries are the dominant energy storage technology for electric vehicles because of their high energy density, long cycle life and superior electrochemical performance. However, battery safety, performance and reliability are strongly influenced by thermal behaviour and the rate of heat generation inside the cells.

During real-world electric vehicle operation, batteries experience continuously varying current loads, temperatures and states of charge. Conventional heat generation estimation approaches are frequently based on simplified assumptions and constant-current conditions, which can limit their applicability under dynamic EV drive cycles.

This doctoral research therefore integrates electrochemical–thermal modelling, direct isothermal battery calorimetry and machine learning-based prediction to develop accurate and computationally efficient methods for battery heat generation estimation.

Research Framework

Three Core Research Pillars

The doctoral research combines physics-based modelling, high-fidelity experimental measurements and artificial intelligence.

01

Electrochemical–Thermal Modelling

A coupled electrochemical–thermal model is developed for a commercial 18650 LFP cell to investigate battery heat generation under realistic dynamic EV operation.

  • FUDS parameter estimation
  • US06 model validation
  • SOC estimation
  • Temperature estimation
  • Bernardi heat evaluation
02

Experimental Battery Calorimetry

Direct calorimetric measurements are used to characterize heat generation behaviour across different battery chemistries, discharge rates and operating temperatures.

  • LFP, NMC and LCO cells
  • 18650 cylindrical cells
  • 0.5C–8C discharge rates
  • 10–60 °C temperature range
  • Thermal efficiency assessment
03

Machine Learning & Symbolic Regression

High-fidelity calorimetric measurements are used to develop computationally efficient predictive models for battery heat generation estimation.

  • FNN & LSTM
  • Support Vector Machine
  • Random Forest Regression
  • KNN & GPR
  • PySR Symbolic Regression
