Battery Modeling Overview

Battery models serve as virtual representations of lithium-ion cells, enabling accurate prediction of electrical, thermal and electrochemical behaviour for Battery Management Systems, digital twins and electric vehicle applications.

Battery Modeling

Why Battery Modeling?

Modern Battery Management Systems require reliable mathematical models to estimate internal battery states that cannot be measured directly. Battery models provide a computational framework for predicting terminal voltage, State of Charge (SOC), temperature, heat generation, degradation and electrochemical dynamics under realistic operating conditions.

Depending on the required balance between computational efficiency and prediction accuracy, battery models can be broadly classified into physics-based electrochemical models and simplified equivalent circuit models. Both approaches are extensively investigated in this research for battery thermal management and advanced state estimation.

Battery Model Classification

Physics-Based Modeling

Pseudo Two-Dimensional (P2D) Electrochemical Model

The Pseudo Two-Dimensional (P2D) model, developed by Doyle, Fuller and Newman, is one of the most comprehensive electrochemical models for lithium-ion batteries. The model simultaneously solves lithium diffusion in solid particles, ionic transport in the electrolyte, charge conservation and electrochemical reaction kinetics occurring at the electrode–electrolyte interface.

Unlike simplified battery models, the P2D framework predicts internal electrochemical variables including lithium concentration, electrolyte concentration, reaction current density, overpotential, terminal voltage and distributed heat generation. Coupling the electrochemical equations with thermal models enables accurate prediction of battery temperature and heat generation during realistic EV drive cycles.

Equivalent Circuit Modeling

Temperature-Dependent Second-Order RC (2RC) Model

Equivalent Circuit Models (ECMs) simplify complex electrochemical behaviour using electrical circuit elements such as resistors, capacitors and voltage sources. Their low computational complexity makes them highly suitable for real-time Battery Management Systems.

In this research, a temperature-dependent second-order Thevenin (2RC) equivalent circuit model was developed using Hybrid Pulse Power Characterization (HPPC) and Open Circuit Voltage (OCV) measurements performed between 15 °C and 45 °C. The model accurately represents instantaneous ohmic behaviour together with short-term and long-term polarization effects, providing reliable voltage prediction under dynamic operating conditions.

Temperature-Dependent Parameter Identification

Accurate equivalent circuit models require reliable model parameters identified under different operating temperatures. Hybrid Pulse Power Characterization (HPPC) and Open Circuit Voltage (OCV) experiments were performed to obtain temperature-dependent electrical parameters and OCV–SOC relationships for the second-order equivalent circuit model.

Experimental Characterization

Hybrid Pulse Power Characterization (HPPC)

Hybrid Pulse Power Characterization (HPPC) experiments were conducted over a temperature range of 15°C to 45°C to identify the temperature-dependent parameters of the second-order equivalent circuit model. The tests capture battery response under dynamic current pulses, enabling accurate estimation of ohmic resistance and polarization parameters.

The identified parameters include instantaneous resistance (R₀), polarization resistances (R₁ and R₂), polarization capacitances (C₁ and C₂), and the Open Circuit Voltage (OCV) curve required for state estimation under varying operating conditions.

OCV-Based Correction

OCV–SOC–Temperature Lookup Table

Equilibrium Open Circuit Voltage (OCV) measurements were performed over the temperature range of 15°C–45°C to construct temperature-dependent lookup tables. These lookup tables provide an accurate relationship between battery State of Charge (SOC), Open Circuit Voltage (OCV), and operating temperature.

The lookup tables are integrated into the Hybrid Adaptive Kalman Filter to correct accumulated estimation drift during battery rest periods, thereby improving long-term SOC prediction accuracy.

Hybrid AKF–OCV–LUT Results

The proposed Hybrid Adaptive Kalman Filter–OCV Lookup Table framework combines model-based estimation with temperature-dependent OCV correction to minimize estimation drift during long-term operation.

Compared with conventional Adaptive Kalman Filter and standalone OCV lookup methods, the proposed framework achieved approximately 39–42% improvement in State-of-Charge estimation accuracy under customized electric vehicle drive cycles.

Battery Aging

Aging-Aware SOC Estimation

Battery degradation alters equivalent circuit parameters, leading to estimation errors if aging is neglected. The proposed framework incorporates aging-induced parameter variation, enabling reliable SOC estimation over the battery lifetime.

Simulation results up to 3000 aging cycles demonstrate stable voltage prediction and robust SOC tracking despite gradual degradation.
