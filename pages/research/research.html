Research Overview

My research integrates experimental battery characterization, electrochemical-thermal modelling, and artificial intelligence to understand, characterize, and predict the behaviour of lithium-ion batteries under realistic operating conditions.

My research focuses on the experimental investigation of lithium-ion battery heat generation and thermal behaviour, supported by physics-based electrochemical and thermal modelling. High-fidelity experimental measurements are obtained through advanced battery calorimetry and controlled environmental testing to investigate battery behaviour under different operating conditions.

The experimental results are further integrated with electrochemical-thermal models to understand the underlying mechanisms of lithium transport, electrochemical reactions, heat generation, and temperature evolution within the battery. These experimentally validated models provide a physics-based framework for analysing battery performance and thermal behaviour.

In parallel, machine learning and artificial intelligence approaches are explored to develop accurate and computationally efficient predictive models for battery heat generation and thermal behaviour. The overall research direction combines experiments, physics-based modelling, and data-driven methods towards safer, more reliable, and intelligent battery systems for electric vehicle and energy-storage applications.

Introduction to Lithium-ion Batteries

Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) have become the dominant energy storage technology because of their high energy density, long cycle life, lightweight design, and excellent charge– discharge efficiency. Their superior performance has enabled widespread adoption across transportation, portable electronics, and grid-scale energy storage applications.

Working Principle

A lithium-ion battery consists of a positive electrode (cathode), a negative electrode (anode), an electrolyte, and a porous separator. During charging, lithium ions move from the cathode to the anode through the electrolyte, while electrons travel through the external circuit. During discharge, the process reverses, delivering electrical energy to the connected load.

The continuous intercalation and deintercalation of lithium ions during charge and discharge are accompanied by electrochemical reactions that generate heat. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for improving battery safety, thermal management, performance, and lifetime.

Applications of Lithium-ion Batteries

Owing to their excellent electrochemical performance, lithium-ion batteries have become the preferred energy storage technology across numerous sectors. Their applications continue to expand with the rapid growth of electrification, renewable energy integration, and portable electronic devices.

Transportation

Lithium-ion batteries are extensively used in electric vehicles (EVs), hybrid electric vehicles, electric buses, aviation, e-bikes, forklifts, and other transportation systems requiring high-power energy storage.

Consumer Electronics

Smartphones, laptops, cameras, wearable devices, Bluetooth accessories, and power tools rely on lithium-ion batteries because of their lightweight design, compact size, and long operating life.

Grid Energy Storage

Grid-scale Energy Storage Systems (ESS), renewable energy integration, backup power systems, and portable energy storage units increasingly employ lithium-ion batteries to improve energy reliability and sustainability.

Why Battery Thermal Management?

Among these applications, electric vehicles represent one of the most thermally demanding operating environments because batteries experience high current loads, rapid charging, regenerative braking, and continuously changing ambient temperatures. These conditions significantly influence battery temperature, safety, degradation, and overall performance, making Battery Thermal Management Systems (BTMS) indispensable for next-generation electric mobility.

Commercial Lithium-Ion Cell Formats

Lithium-ion batteries are manufactured in different cell formats depending on the application requirements. Each configuration offers unique advantages in terms of energy density, thermal behaviour, manufacturing complexity, mechanical strength, and thermal management.

🔋 Cylindrical Cells

Cylindrical cells provide excellent mechanical stability, uniform heat dissipation, and high manufacturing consistency. They are widely used in electric vehicles and constitute the primary focus of this research. Common commercial formats include 18650, 21700, and 4680 cells.

🟦 Prismatic Cells

Prismatic cells provide high volumetric energy density and superior packaging efficiency, making them suitable for electric vehicles, stationary energy storage systems, and compact battery pack designs.

📄 Pouch Cells

Pouch cells are lightweight and offer excellent gravimetric energy density. Their flexible packaging enables efficient space utilization but requires additional mechanical support because of cell swelling during operation.

🪙 Coin Cells

Coin (button) cells are primarily used for laboratory research, electrochemical characterization, and material development. They provide a convenient and economical platform for evaluating new electrode materials and electrolytes before scaling to commercial cylindrical, prismatic, or pouch cells.

Effective thermal management is essential for ensuring the safety, performance, durability, and fast-charging capability of lithium-ion batteries used in electric vehicles.

Heat Generation Mechanisms

Heat generation inside lithium-ion batteries arises from multiple electrochemical and physical processes during charging and discharging. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for accurate thermal modelling, Battery Management Systems (BMS), and the design of efficient Battery Thermal Management Systems (BTMS).

⚡ Irreversible (Joule) Heat

  • Generated due to internal resistance.
  • Dominant heat source at high current rates.
  • Proportional to current squared (I²R).

🌡 Reversible (Entropic) Heat

  • Depends on entropy change during electrochemical reactions.
  • Can be endothermic or exothermic.
  • Strongly influenced by State of Charge (SOC).

🧪 Reaction Heat

  • Associated with electrochemical reaction kinetics.
  • Influenced by overpotential and polarization.
  • Becomes significant during high-rate operation.

📈 Research Focus

  • Experimental heat generation measurement.
  • Electrochemical-thermal modelling.
  • AI-based heat prediction under EV drive cycles.

Bernardi Heat Generation Equation

The total heat generation rate inside a lithium-ion battery is commonly estimated using the Bernardi equation:

Q = I(V − U) − IT(dU/dT)

where I is the current, V is the terminal voltage, U is the open-circuit voltage, T is the battery temperature, and dU/dT represents the entropic heat coefficient.

Methods to Determine Heat Generation Rate (HGR)

Heat Generation Rate (HGR) can be determined using direct experimental measurements, physics-based electrochemical models, equivalent circuit models, empirical equations, or data-driven machine learning techniques. Each approach provides unique advantages depending on the required prediction accuracy, computational efficiency, and experimental complexity.

Direct Measurement

Heat generation is measured directly using calorimetric techniques such as isothermal and adiabatic calorimeters.

Electrochemical Models

Physics-based models estimate heat generation from electrochemical reactions occurring inside the battery.

Equivalent Circuit Models

Electrical equivalent circuits provide efficient real-time estimation of battery thermal behaviour for Battery Management Systems.

Bernardi Equation

One of the most widely used analytical methods for estimating battery heat generation from electrical and thermodynamic parameters.

Machine Learning

Data-driven algorithms learn complex nonlinear relationships directly from experimental data for accurate real-time HGR prediction.

Experimental Facility

Accurate characterization of battery heat generation is essential for developing reliable Battery Thermal Management Systems (BTMS). This research employs two complementary calorimetric techniques for battery thermal characterization and safety evaluation.

🔬 Isothermal Battery Calorimeter (HEL ISO BTC+)

The HEL ISO BTC+ maintains the battery under a constant ambient temperature while directly measuring the heat released or absorbed during charge and discharge. Unlike electrical estimation methods, this technique provides direct and highly reliable Heat Generation Rate (HGR) measurements, making it an ideal tool for validating electrochemical-thermal models and developing machine learning algorithms.

Numerical Models

Numerical models provide an effective framework for understanding, predicting, and optimizing lithium-ion battery behaviour. Depending on the desired balance between prediction accuracy and computational efficiency, battery models can be broadly classified into detailed physics-based electrochemical models and simplified equivalent circuit models. Both approaches are employed in this research to investigate battery thermal behaviour and heat generation under realistic operating conditions.

Physics-Based Battery Modelling

Pseudo Two-Dimensional (P2D) Electrochemical Model

The Pseudo Two-Dimensional (P2D) electrochemical model, developed by Doyle, Fuller, and Newman, is one of the most comprehensive physics-based models for lithium-ion batteries. It simultaneously describes lithium diffusion within active materials, ionic transport through the electrolyte, charge conservation, and electrochemical reaction kinetics occurring at the electrode-electrolyte interface.

Unlike simplified electrical models, the P2D model predicts internal electrochemical states including lithium concentration, electrolyte concentration, reaction current density, overpotential, terminal voltage, and spatial heat generation. In this research, a coupled electrochemical-thermal model was developed and validated using experimentally measured calorimetric data for predicting heat generation under constant-current and dynamic EV drive cycles.

Physics-Based Model

Represents electrochemical processes inside the battery.

Electrochemical Reactions

Predicts lithium diffusion and reaction kinetics.

Thermal Coupling

Predicts voltage, temperature and heat generation simultaneously.

Drive Cycle Simulation

Suitable for realistic EV operating conditions.

Equivalent Circuit Cell Model (ECM)

Thevenin Second-Order RC (2RC) Model

Equivalent Circuit Models (ECMs) simplify complex electrochemical phenomena using electrical components such as voltage sources, resistors, and capacitors. Their low computational complexity makes them highly suitable for real-time Battery Management Systems (BMS).

In this research, a second-order Thevenin (2RC) model was developed to capture both instantaneous ohmic behaviour and transient polarization effects. Temperature-dependent model parameters were experimentally identified to improve voltage prediction accuracy under varying operating conditions.

Real-Time BMS

Suitable for online battery state estimation.

Fast Computation

Very low computational cost compared with P2D models.

Voltage Prediction

Accurately predicts battery terminal voltage.

Temperature Dependent

Model parameters vary with operating temperature.

Machine Learning-Based Heat Prediction

Machine learning techniques were employed to develop accurate predictive models for battery Heat Generation Rate (HGR) using experimentally measured calorimetric data. The developed models utilize current, C-rate, Depth of Discharge (DOD), and temperature as input features to predict battery heat generation under a wide range of operating conditions.

Machine Learning Pipeline

A comprehensive machine learning workflow was developed to predict Heat Generation Rate (HGR) directly from experimentally measured battery data. The workflow consists of

  • Battery data acquisition
  • Data preprocessing and normalization
  • Training-validation-test split
  • Hyperparameter optimization
  • Model evaluation using RMSE, MAE and R²
  • Feature importance analysis (SHAP)
  • Symbolic Regression using PySR
  • Development of analytical HGR correlations

The proposed framework combines machine learning prediction with symbolic regression, enabling accurate and computationally efficient real-time implementation in Battery Management Systems.

Machine Learning Algorithms

Feed Forward Neural Network (FNN)

Feed Forward Neural Networks (FNN) learn nonlinear relationships between battery operating parameters and heat generation through interconnected hidden layers. Their strong approximation capability enables accurate prediction of battery Heat Generation Rate from experimental calorimetric datasets.

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)

LSTM networks are specifically designed for sequential battery data by capturing temporal dependencies through memory cells. They provide excellent prediction capability during dynamic operating conditions such as electric vehicle drive cycles.

Support Vector Machine (SVM)

Support Vector Machine constructs an optimal hyperplane in a transformed feature space to achieve robust nonlinear regression. SVM provides excellent generalization capability while minimizing overfitting for medium-sized experimental datasets.

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

KNN predicts battery Heat Generation Rate by identifying neighbouring experimental samples with similar operating conditions. The prediction is obtained from the weighted average of nearby samples, making KNN simple, interpretable, and effective for nonlinear battery datasets.

Random Forest Regression (RFR)

Random Forest Regression combines predictions from multiple decision trees to improve prediction accuracy and reduce variance. Ensemble learning enables highly robust prediction of battery Heat Generation Rate across different operating conditions.

Developed Heat Generation Correlation

Beyond machine learning prediction, this research developed explicit analytical Heat Generation Rate (HGR) correlations using Symbolic Regression (PySR Genetic Algorithm). Unlike conventional black-box models, these correlations can be directly implemented in Battery Management Systems for real-time heat estimation.

HGR = f(C-rate, Current, DOD, Temperature)

The developed correlations were trained using experimentally measured calorimetric datasets and validated over a wide range of operating temperatures and discharge rates. They provide computationally efficient alternatives to electrochemical models while maintaining excellent prediction accuracy.
